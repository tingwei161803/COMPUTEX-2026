Keynote Highlights

The key announcements from Huang's keynote. Tap a card for the full take.

Vera Rubin NVL72 Enters Full Production

At the GTC Taipei / COMPUTEX 2026 keynote (June 1, 2026, Taipei), Jensen Huang announced that the next-generation rack-scale AI supercomputer Vera Rubin NVL72 is now in full production.

Vera CPU: First Standalone Data Center Processor Built for AI Agents

Huang unveiled NVIDIA's first standalone data center CPU, 'Vera,' stressing that past CPUs were built for humans while Vera is purpose-built for AI agents.

The Agentic AI Era Has Arrived: 'Every Token Is Revenue'

The keynote's central thesis was that agentic AI is here, it works, and it makes money.

RTX Spark and N1X Superchip: NVIDIA's Full-Scale Entry into the AI PC Market

Huang announced that NVIDIA and Microsoft will 'reinvent the PC,' introducing the RTX Spark superchip (containing a processor named N1X) co-developed with MediaTek on TSMC's 3nm process.

Nemotron 3 Ultra Open Model and the DSX AI Factory Platform

Huang introduced the open model Nemotron 3 Ultra, a 550-billion-parameter hybrid of state-space models and mixture-of-experts that runs up to 5x faster and about 30% cheaper than leading open models, with frontier-level intelligence.

Investing in Taiwan: The Constellation Campus and a Vast Supply Chain

Huang announced that construction is about to begin on NVIDIA's new Taipei R&D headquarters, the 'Constellation' campus, in the Beitou-Shilin Technology Park—about 4 hectares, designed to house roughly 4,000 employees and echoing the iconic look of the Santa Clara headquarters, expected to be operational by 2030.

Physical AI and Robotics: Jetson Thor, Cosmos 3, and the Isaac GR00T Humanoid

On physical AI and robotics, Huang introduced the edge AI platform Jetson Thor (2,070 FP4 teraflops, a 7.5x compute increase over Jetson Orin, configurable 40-130 watts, winner of a COMPUTEX Golden Award), the open foundation model Cosmos 3 for physical AI, and the first open humanoid robot reference design, the Isaac GR00T Reference Humanoid (built on Jetson Thor and the Isaac GR00T platform).

Market Reaction: NVIDIA and AI Infrastructure Partners Rally

On the day of the keynote, NVIDIA stock rose about 6% and the S&P 500 closed at a record high.

Market Read

Where industry and capital are moving after the keynote.

"Compute Is Revenue": Huang Declares Agentic AI Has Arrived, Wall Street Rallies

At his GTC Taipei / COMPUTEX 2026 keynote, Jensen Huang declared that "agentic AI has arrived, useful AI has arrived," repeatedly arguing that "compute is revenue." He framed AI as no longer experimental but a practical productivity tool that generates real ROI for companies. The pitch resonated with Wall Street: NVIDIA stock jumped roughly 6% on the day, helping lift the broader market.

AI Capex at Scale: A 1GW Data Center Is ~$40B, Spawning More Winners

After the keynote, analysts re-anchored the scale of AI infrastructure spend: a 1GW data center now represents about $40 billion of capex, of which NVIDIA addresses $20–30 billion depending on networking content. Jim Cramer argued the keynote showed the AI boom is minting winners well beyond NVIDIA, naming Arm, Oracle, Nebius and CoreWeave; Dell rose more than 10% and HP gained about 8% on the day.

Vera Rubin Enters Full Production: AI Server Supply Chain Twice the Size of Grace Blackwell

NVIDIA announced its Vera Rubin platform is ramping into full production to power agentic AI factories worldwide, with a supply chain about twice the size of Grace Blackwell. Its flagship Vera Rubin NVL72 rack-scale system won multiple Best Choice Awards at COMPUTEX 2026; HBM4 memory is supplied by Samsung, SK hynix and Micron, with first customer shipments slated for this summer and broad availability in the second half of 2026.

Physical AI and Humanoids: Open Isaac GR00T Reference Design, Partnering with Unitree

NVIDIA pushed "physical AI" toward the real world, unveiling an open Isaac GR00T humanoid reference design that pairs a Unitree H2 Plus chassis, Sharpa five-fingered hands and NVIDIA Jetson Thor onboard compute (Blackwell GPU, 128GB unified memory). Institutions including Ai2, ETH Zurich, the Stanford Robotics Center and UC San Diego will adopt it, with availability from Unitree expected in late 2026.

Sovereign AI and Taiwan as "AI Capital": NVIDIA Pledges $150B a Year, Breaks Ground on Constellation

Huang called Taiwan the "epicentre" of the AI revolution and publicly stated NVIDIA's annual spending in Taiwan will reach roughly $150 billion—the highest figure he had cited, exceeding the GDP of most EU member states. NVIDIA also unveiled "Constellation," a new Taiwan headquarters campus set to break ground in June or July 2026 and reach full operation by 2030, housing about 4,000 direct employees and generating more than 10,000 jobs overall.

Semiconductor Supply Chain Strained: TSMC Works Overtime on Rubin, ~2M Parts Stress the Ecosystem

Huang called Vera Rubin "probably the largest product launch in the history of Taiwan," with each system containing nearly 2 million parts built by roughly 150 ecosystem partners on the island. TSMC fabricates the advanced logic while Foxconn, Quanta and Wistron handle assembly. Reports note the Rubin ramp is already straining the ecosystem, with TSMC working overtime to meet the order book—underscoring Taiwan's irreplaceable role in the AI hardware supply chain.

Taiwan's Role: TAIEX Jumps 1,000+ Points Above 45,000 as ODM and Brand Stocks Hit Limit-Up

Huang's keynote and COMPUTEX optimism drove the TAIEX up more than 1,000 points in early trade, pushing the index above 45,000 for the first time and to an intraday record of 45,931. TSMC, Foxconn, MediaTek and Quanta all hit all-time highs, while benchmark names such as Asus, Compal, Acer, Quanta and Wistron locked limit-up shortly after the open—reflecting strong expectations that Taiwan's AI supply chain will be a prime beneficiary.

Forecasts & Outlook

Forward-looking claims from NVIDIA, analysts and media — predictions that may change.

  1. Roadmap

    [Prediction/Outlook] Vera Rubin Enters Production, Rubin Ultra to Follow in H2 2027

    In his GTC Taipei / COMPUTEX 2026 keynote (June 1, 11:00 a.m. Taiwan time), Jensen Huang announced that the next-gen AI infrastructure platform Vera Rubin is 'in full production,' succeeding the Blackwell architecture. On the forward roadmap, reporting indicates Rubin racks are expected to ship in H2 2026, with the Rubin R100 GPU sampling with select customers in Q4 2026 and volume production beginning Q1 2027; the Rubin CPX (8 exaflops) configuration arrives at the end of 2026, with Rubin Ultra (15 exaflops FP4) slated for H2 2027. The supply chain is described as twice the size of Grace Blackwell. Note these shipment dates and timelines are forward-looking plans and may shift.

  2. Shipments

    [Prediction/Outlook] Analysts Model ~30,000 Rubin Units in 2026, 100,000 in 2027

    With the production timeline set, analyst models project Rubin system shipments of roughly 30,000 units in 2026, rising to about 100,000 in 2027. Reporting also cites each NVL72 server requiring roughly 1,152 TB of NAND flash, rippling into the memory supply chain. NVIDIA says its supply chain can build thousands of racks per week, but for Vera Rubin systems, customers not already in the procurement pipeline (commitments typically locked 6–12 months ahead of delivery) could face 2027 delivery dates. These are third-party analyst forward estimates, not guaranteed figures.

  3. 2027展望

    [Prediction/Outlook] NVIDIA Raises Demand Visibility to $1 Trillion Through 2027

    Alongside the Vera Rubin reveal, NVIDIA raised its demand visibility from a previously forecasted $500 billion through 2026 to $1 trillion through 2027, driven by surging inference demand. Huang reiterated his view that this is only the start—he has projected total hyperscaler capex heading toward the '$3 to $4 trillion' mark. Reporting notes the keynote framed 'useful AI has arrived' and AI as a 'profit generator.' This is a company forward-looking outlook; actual revenue is subject to earnings reports.

  4. Capex

    [Prediction/Outlook] Big-5 Hyperscaler Capex ~$725B in 2026, Approaching $1T in 2027

    On the market outlook, aggregations after Q1 2026 earnings put combined 2026 capex for the Big-5 hyperscalers (Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta, Oracle) at roughly $725 billion (earlier estimates exceeded $600 billion, with ~75%, or $450B, aimed at AI infrastructure), with analysts expecting hyperscale capex to top $1 trillion in 2027. Goldman Sachs projects total 2025–2027 hyperscaler capex of about $1.15 trillion and, in its baseline, sees annual AI capex growing from ~$765 billion in 2026 to $1.6 trillion by 2031. These are analyst and investment-bank forward estimates.

  5. Robotics

    [Prediction/Outlook] Physical AI Timeline: Isaac GR00T Humanoid by Late 2026, Level 4 Robotaxis as Soon as 2027

    Huang declared 'the era of physical AI is approaching,' formally entering the physical AI business and unveiling the Cosmos 3 world model plus open-source robotics tools. On the roadmap: the NVIDIA Isaac GR00T Reference Humanoid Robot (built on Jetson Thor) is expected from Unitree in late 2026, with the Unitree H2 Plus also arriving late 2026; the next-gen vision-language-action model GR00T N2 is claimed to rank #1 on both MolmoSpaces and RoboArena benchmarks. In autonomy, NVIDIA aims for partners to deploy Level 4 robotaxis using its chips and DRIVE stack as soon as 2027; Foxconn, VinFast, Uber, and HUMAIN have joined the DRIVE Hyperion ecosystem, with Foxconn planning a robotaxi service in Taiwan in 2028. These are forward-looking product-launch and deployment plans.

  6. AI PC

    [Prediction/Outlook] RTX Spark Pushes 'Personal AI' PCs, Shipping Fall 2026 with a Three-Generation Roadmap

    NVIDIA expanded its Microsoft partnership and unveiled the RTX Spark superchip to push Windows toward an era of 'personal AI agents' (running 120B-parameter LLMs locally with up to 1M-token context). Outlook: RTX Spark slim laptops and compact desktops are expected to ship in fall 2026 from ASUS, Dell, HP, Lenovo, Microsoft Surface and MSI, with Acer and GIGABYTE to follow; Microsoft also introduced a Surface RTX Spark Dev Box for developers. NVIDIA further outlined a three-generation RTX Spark roadmap—first Grace Blackwell, then an LPDDR6 Rubin generation, followed by Rosa Feynman. These are forward-looking product-launch dates and multi-generation plans.

  7. 市場反應

    [Prediction/Outlook] Stock Climbs Post-Keynote; Analyst Targets Diverge from $255 to $350

    Market reaction and outlook: reporting notes NVIDIA shares rose more than 6% on the Monday of keynote week and kept climbing in Tuesday premarket. Analyst price targets diverged—the 12-month average sits around $309.94 (implying ~38% upside); one analyst set a $350 target on a 26x ex-cash 2027 P/E, DA Davidson reiterated Buy at $300, and Goldman Sachs reiterated Buy at $285 (citing NVIDIA's push with Microsoft into the traditional PC market), while Deutsche Bank's Ross Seymore maintained a Hold and $255 target after the event. These are analyst forward ratings and targets, not investment advice.

Sentiment Analysis

How the market reads it — optimism and skepticism side by side.

On June 1, 2026, Jensen Huang delivered a roughly two-hour GTC Taipei / COMPUTEX 2026 keynote whose core theses were that agentic AI has arrived and makes money, and that 'compute is revenue.' He confirmed the Vera Rubin platform is in full production, unveiled the Vera CPU, and announced the RTX Spark push into AI PCs. The overall sentiment skewed bullish: NVDA rose about 2.7% after hours, Taiwan's market jumped over 1,000 points in early trading with the index breaking 45,600 to a record high, and TSMC, Foxconn, MediaTek and Quanta all hit all-time highs. At the same time, a clear skeptical chorus emerged, led by Michael Burry flagging circular financing, stretched valuations and AI-bubble risk.

The Bull Case

  • 'Agentic AI has arrived and makes money' became the central narrative: Huang argued the chatbot era is over and that systems which set goals, break tasks into steps, call tools and act autonomously are here, repeatedly stressing 'compute is revenue' with every token as a revenue unit.
  • The Vera Rubin AI computing platform was confirmed in full production at staggering scale: seven custom chips, six trillion transistors, more than 18,000 components on a single board and over 1.3 million components in total, with first customers including Anthropic, OpenAI and SpaceX.
  • The 'Huang effect' ignited Taiwan's supply chain: after the keynote Taiwan's market jumped over 1,000 points in early trading and the index broke 45,600 to a record high, with TSMC, Foxconn, MediaTek and Quanta all hitting all-time highs; NVDA rose about 2.7% after hours in the US.
  • RTX Spark was framed as NVIDIA's 'paradigm shift' from component supplier to PC architecture owner: the Arm-based laptop chip simultaneously challenges Apple (M5), Intel, Qualcomm and AMD, with Forrester analyst Charlie Dai saying it will directly raise competitive pressure.
  • TV commentator Jim Cramer said the keynote showed AI-infrastructure winners well beyond NVIDIA, naming Arm Holdings, Oracle, Nebius and CoreWeave; TradingKey analysis noted Arm surged about 15% on its royalty model and called TSMC a confirmed beneficiary, expected to take roughly 60% of global CoWoS capacity in 2026.
  • NVIDIA painted an enormous AI opportunity: Huang declared 'the AI market will be a hundred times larger than before,' the company has pointed to a $3–4 trillion AI market, and ahead of COMPUTEX reports of a roughly $150 billion Taiwan-related investment/spending plan further lifted Taiwan chip stocks.

The Bear Case

  • Circular-financing concerns: Michael Burry called NVIDIA's xAI/SpaceX-related chip deal 'fugazi,' arguing NVIDIA injected about $1.9 billion into Valor, the vehicle buying its own chips, effectively helping fund a buyer from which it then booked about $5.4 billion in revenue, and warned of concentration risk in GPUs leased to a single customer.
  • Valuation and the 'platform company' narrative remain unproven: TradingKey noted the upgrade from chip company to AI platform company 'won't be numerically validated until 2027,' yet NVIDIA is already priced at platform multiples of 35–45x P/E; consensus target is about $298 (range $250–$325), a bet on future execution.
  • AI bubble and capital concentration: Burry cited data that about 87% of venture funding now goes to AI (versus under 40% tied to internet firms in 1999) and likened NVIDIA to late-1990s Cisco, a genuine pick-and-shovel provider whose valuation may detach from fundamentals; commentary keeps debating whether the 2026 bull market is real AI productivity or mega-cap speculative concentration.
  • Energy and grid bottlenecks: global data-center critical IT power is projected to rise from 49GW in 2023 to 96GW by 2026 (about 40GW for AI), with NVIDIA's Rubin racks eventually needing about 300kW and the industry approaching 1MW per rack; power buildout lags demand, pressuring electricity prices and potentially slowing the boom, with only about a third of 2026's planned 12GW capacity under construction.
  • Geopolitics and catching-up rivals: China export restrictions and competition from local players like Alibaba pose risks; Huang estimated a roughly $50 billion China opportunity in 2026 if addressable with competitive products, but it remains constrained, while projects like Google/Meta's TorchTPU aim to erode CUDA lock-in, though even optimistically that would take 12–18 months to replace in production.
